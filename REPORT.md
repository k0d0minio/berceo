# Berceo / "Gardiennes de la Nuit": Technical, Business & Effort Assessment for a Prospective Solo Developer

## TL;DR
- **Take the project only on a phased, day-rate basis with a tightly scoped V1 — do not accept a fixed-price "build the whole cahier des charges" deal.** The spec is internally contradictory, the founders are non-technical, subscription prices are undefined, and the core revenue model (booking fee + cash-off-platform settlement) is structurally exposed to disintermediation. As written, the full annotated scope is roughly a 90–140 developer-day build; a defensible, launchable V1 is ~45–70 days.
- **The two hard external dependencies are itsme and the Belgian regulatory context, and both can stall launch independently of your code.** itsme has no published price, needs a registered legal entity and OV/EV certificates, and realistically takes "under 3 months" to go live; the overnight-infant-care activity itself brushes against ONE/Kind & Gezin childcare rules, self-employment/undeclared-work exposure, and the EU Platform Work Directive (transposition due 2 December 2026).
- **Where you add the most value is not coding — it is scoping/phasing, regulatory-aware product decisions, AI-assisted document review for diploma verification, and building the admin/ops tooling the founders will live in.** Price and position yourself as an architect/product engineer, not a feature-taker.

## Key Findings

**Market.** Belgium recorded **108,150 births in 2024 — the lowest since 1942** (Statbel: "With 108,150 births in 2024, the birth rate in Belgium registers a decrease of 1.9% compared to 2023 (110,198 births)… the lowest registered since 1942"), split 61,781 Flanders / 32,539 Wallonia / 13,830 Brussels, with the total fertility rate at 1.44 (Brussels 1.34, Wallonia 1.43, Flanders 1.48) and declining. A French-first V1 effectively targets Wallonia + francophone Brussels, i.e. a birth pool of roughly 40,000–46,000/year, a **shrinking** base. Postnatal home care in Belgium is already partly organised and reimbursed: midwife home visits are covered by INAMI (code 428735 for post-J6 surveillance was raised ~50% on 1 Jan 2026 to roughly €68 non-BIM), and in Flanders the structured "kraamzorg" system provides postnatal help. On the francophone side the nearest equivalent is the doula, whose care is only partially reimbursed — some mutualités (notably Helan and LM/Mutplus) offer a flat intervention of up to ~€150 per parent. **The critical business insight: the paid "night nanny" niche exists mostly in the gap left by the reimbursed system, and competes against heavily subsidised alternatives.**

**Existing/adjacent players.** No dominant francophone-Belgian "night nanny marketplace" was found — a genuine white space, but likely because demand is thin, not untapped. Adjacent competitors:
- **Bsit** — Belgium's leading babysitting platform since 2015, with **"over 3,328 active babysitters in Brussels"** (avg 9–12€/hr). It already offers **optional itsme® and Stripe ID checks**, in-app pay, subscriptions, per-booking insurance, and a strong local-density claim: *"Bsit always recommends babysitters in your area first, active within the last 2 weeks. 75% of parents find a matching Sitter within 500m."* Bsit explicitly states it is **not** a collaborative-economy platform and that parents must hold *"Gens de Maison / Huispersoneel"* insurance. **This is essentially the competitor that could add night care with one feature flag** and is the most likely competitive threat.
- **Care.com** (operating in Belgium, subscription model), **Babysits**, **Yoopies**, and **Ring Twice** (one of the first *agréé* collaborative-economy platforms in Belgium).
- In Flanders/NL, specialist night-care agencies exist (e.g. Nannies at Night, The Night Nanny) running an **agency** model where the platform charges a service fee and the parent pays the nanny directly after the shift — strikingly close to Berceo's intended model. Belgian night-rate reference points: the Gezinsbond childcare service charges €25/night (22h–8h); specialist agencies price nannies at ~€15–21/hour. The €100–300/night slider is plausible for a premium, professionalised offer but sits well above the subsidised baseline.

**Business-model risk (disintermediation) — the model's central flaw.** Academic and industry literature is unanimous that leakage is worst exactly where Berceo sits: **service marketplaces** (healthy take rates 15–30%), **high-value recurring relationships**, and — critically — where **the platform does NOT process the core payment**. Berceo intends to collect only a booking service fee while the actual €100–300/night is settled off-platform in cash/QR/transfer. Night-nanny relationships recur over weeks, and Care.com's own model shows the pattern: the marketplace's core product "is the introduction, not the ongoing relationship. Once a family finds their nanny… the family often lets the membership lapse. This is a business built on churn." The Care.com subscription-for-access template is the *right* pattern for a low-frequency, high-trust intro service — but it means Berceo's real revenue is the introduction fee + subscriptions, and it must **accept that repeat bookings will leak off-platform**. The booking-fee + tiered family subscriptions + pro subscription + gated-profiles stack is coherent *if and only if* subscriptions and the first booking fee are priced to capture value up front. The danger is expecting recurring transaction revenue that will evaporate.

**Regulatory context (materially affects viability).**
- *Childcare licensing.* In francophone Belgium the ONE requires anyone who "organises regularly the reception of children aged 0–12" outside the family to file a prior declaration and comply with a Code de Qualité; in Flanders all childcare initiatives must be notified to Kind & Gezin/Opgroeien and are inspected (independent minders capped at 7 children; up to 8 in accredited services). Whether one-off overnight in-home newborn care by a self-employed professional triggers full accreditation is genuinely uncertain, but the platform cannot assume exemption. This must be legally checked before launch and directly shapes the verification feature.
- *Platform tax regime.* Belgium's "économie collaborative" regime (art. 90 CIR; RD of 12 Jan 2017) gives favourable treatment (income taxed as "revenus divers," ~10% withheld at source, effective rate ~10.7% = 10% federal + 0.7% communal on gross after a 50% forfait) BUT requires the platform to be *agréé* and — decisively — that "the remuneration for services is paid or attributed to the service provider BY the platform (or through it)." **Berceo's cash-off-platform model means it cannot use this regime**, because money never flows through it. The 2026 ceiling is **€7,890/year** across all complementary activities (SPF Finances); exceeding it requalifies **all** such income as professional, forcing self-employed (complémentaire) registration. VAT franchise applies below ~€5,000 turnover.
- *Undeclared-work exposure.* Because Berceo facilitates but does not process payment, it risks becoming a channel for "travail au noir" — it surfaces supply and demand, cash changes hands, nothing is reported. This is a reputational and potentially legal liability, and the flip side of the disintermediation flaw. (Note Bsit's deliberate positioning that it is *not* a collaborative-economy platform — a signal that this classification is contested and consequential.)
- *DAC7.* DAC7 (Directive 2021/514) requires platforms facilitating "personal services" to collect/verify and report seller income annually (by 31 January). A key nuance: platforms that only list/advertise and do NOT facilitate the transaction or know its value may fall outside DAC7 — Berceo's off-platform payment could keep it out of DAC7 reporting, but this is double-edged (out of DAC7 often means out of the favourable collaborative-economy regime too). Get this professionally confirmed.
- *EU Platform Work Directive (2024/2831).* Adopted **23 October 2024**, in force **1 December 2024**, transposition due **2 December 2026** (the employment presumption "shall apply only to the period starting from" that date). It introduces a rebuttable presumption of employment when the platform exercises "control and direction," shifting the burden of proof onto the platform. Berceo's light-touch model (pros set their own rate within the slider, no algorithmic dispatch) argues for genuine self-employment, but the €100–300 price *bounding* and matching logic are exactly the "control" signals to keep minimal.

**Trust & safety / liability.** A platform matching strangers with newborns overnight carries acute liability. Care.com was fined by the US FTC and repeatedly criticised over the real-world value of its vetting; the lesson is that a "we verify but don't guarantee" disclaimer reduces but does not eliminate exposure, and Belgian/EU consumer law will not let a platform contract away all responsibility for services it actively arranges and profits from. Bsit's Belgian approach is instructive: it requires parents to hold *"Gens de Maison / Huispersoneel"* insurance and covers each booking with insurance. Berceo should budget for platform liability/RC professionnelle insurance and require proof of the professional's own RC insurance as an onboarding document.

**itsme (the big technical unknown).** itsme is standard OpenID Connect (Authorization Code flow, `private_key_jwt`), with **>80% adoption of Belgium's adult population** (~7M users). Findings:
- *Entity requirement:* No published rule mandates a Belgian KBO number specifically — the onboarding form asks for "organisation details as shown on the company register **for your jurisdiction**." However, itsme requires the partner's site to use **Organisation Validation or Extended Validation TLS certificates** (Let's Encrypt DV is *explicitly insufficient*), and OV/EV issuance requires a verified registered legal entity. So Berceo must be a properly incorporated company (a Belgian company is the natural fit).
- *Pricing:* itsme publishes **NO euro figures**. Official language: *"Our pricing is set up as a gradually decreasing bracketed pricing… depend[ing] on the number of Active Users per Project. Contact our sales experts."* It is a fixed annual price tied to active users. The only concrete numbers found are indirect and NOT representative of a startup's ID/auth rate (a Belgian reseller charges €5.00 excl. VAT for an itsme e-signature; a 2022 government pricing dispute referenced a ~€450,000 cap). Treat itsme cost as an unknown to be quoted by their sales team.
- *Lead time & route:* itsme returns a client_id/service_code within ~3 days of the intake form; full go-live is realistically **"under 3 months"** (Onegini: *"The technical implementation of itsme is straightforward; companies can be up and running in under 3 months. The software uses standard APIs"*), once contract, T&Cs, OV/EV certs and E2E testing are done. Direct OIDC integration is supported, but small startups often go via an **aggregator** (Signicat, Onewelcome/Onegini, Connective, Ometa, or Microsoft Azure AD B2C's built-in connector) that adds margin but removes contractual/compliance friction and offers multiple eIDs behind one integration.
- *Fallback:* The spec's "itsme if possible, otherwise ID card" is wise. For document/ID verification, mainstream KYC vendors publish per-check pricing: **Stripe Identity ~$1.50, Sumsub ~$1.35–1.50 (with ~$149/mo minimum), Veriff from ~$0.80–1.39, Onfido ~$2.25 with a ~$15,000/yr minimum.** None verify *diplomas* — that is a bespoke document-review problem.
- *EUDI Wallet:* The EU Digital Identity Wallet must be available in every member state by **24 December 2026** (per Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/798), with private-sector acceptance obligations ~one year later (~Dec 2027); Belgium's vehicle is MyGov.be/BOSA and it sits in the pilot/second-tier readiness group. **It is NOT a reliable near-term substitute for itsme in 2026** — no production Belgian wallet exists to integrate against yet. Build itsme/KYC as primary, EUDI as a planned later method.

**Stripe architecture.** Because Berceo does NOT pay professionals, **Stripe Connect is not needed** — a major simplification. You need: Stripe Payments (booking service fee) + Stripe Billing (family tiered subscriptions + annual pro subscription) + Belgian/EU payment methods (Bancontact, SEPA, cards natively supported; iDEAL if targeting NL later). **Gift cards are NOT native to Stripe**; realistic approaches: (a) a custom credit-ledger in your own DB (recommended for control), (b) promotion codes / customer balance for simple cases, or (c) a third-party (Gift Up, Cardivo) bridged via Checkout promotion codes. EU VAT on the service fee and subscriptions (B2C electronically-supplied services) means OSS registration and Stripe Tax are worth enabling early.

**Maps / geosearch.** Google Maps restructured pricing in **March 2025**: the flat $200/month credit was replaced by per-SKU free caps (~10,000 dynamic map loads free) and subscription tiers (Starter ~$100/mo for 50k, Essentials ~$275/mo for 100k, Pro ~$1,200/mo for 250k). **Mapbox is ~46% cheaper at scale** (50,000 free web loads, then $5/1,000; geocoding $0.75 vs Google's ~$5 per 1,000). **MapLibre + OpenStreetMap/Protomaps/MapTiler tiles is effectively free of API fees** (you pay in DevOps). For a Belgian MVP with modest traffic, **Mapbox or MapLibre is the pragmatic choice**; use free Belgian address data (BeSt Address / geopunt.be) for geocoding to avoid per-call fees. **Privacy: never plot exact home pins** — follow Airbnb/Rover practice and show an approximation circle/fuzzed centroid until a booking is confirmed. Radius search: **PostGIS on Supabase** (`ST_DWithin` on `geography`) is the clean choice, superior to MongoDB geospatial for this relational, multi-role app.

**Stack recommendation.** **Next.js (App Router) + Supabase (Postgres + Auth + RLS + Storage)** is the right fit over Node/MongoDB: Supabase Row-Level Security gives database-enforced role isolation (family/pro/admin) — valuable for a multi-role marketplace handling sensitive data — plus PostGIS for geosearch and Storage for documents in one platform. Realtime messaging: **Supabase Realtime** suffices for request-scoped threads (Pusher/Ably only if you outgrow it). Transactional email with EU residency: **Resend, Postmark, or Brevo** (Brevo is EU-based). Sensitive identity/diploma docs: EU-region bucket, encryption at rest, strict RLS, explicit retention + deletion workflow. CMS for vitrine content: **Sanity or Payload**, or simply MDX for zero extra infra. **Data-residency caveat:** Vercel and Supabase both offer EU regions (Frankfurt), but "hosted in Europe" is only true if you explicitly select EU regions and check subprocessor DPAs — Vercel is a US company (US CLOUD Act exposure) even when compute is in the EU. For strict guarantees, consider Scaleway/Hetzner/Fly.io EU, at the cost of more ops burden for a solo dev.

**GDPR.** You will process newborn ages and "family context." **Health data is special-category (Art. 9)** — if the app captures anything health-related (e.g. an infant's medical needs), that triggers heightened requirements; keep V1 fields minimal to avoid this. You need: a records-of-processing register; DPAs with every subprocessor (Supabase, Stripe, itsme/KYC vendor, email, maps); retention + deletion workflows (especially for identity/diploma docs); and timestamped, versioned CGU/privacy consent (store consent version + timestamp per user). Belgian cookie/consent-banner rules (GDPR + ePrivacy, APD/GBA guidance) apply.

**SEO vs paywall contradiction.** The client wants public, SEO-indexable professional profile pages AND wants profiles visible only to paying subscribers. **These are mutually exclusive for the profile content itself:** Google cannot index what it cannot see, and cloaking violates Google guidelines. The workable pattern: index public *marketing* pages and per-commune landing pages (programmatic-SEO local pages, mindful of Google's helpful-content stance against thin doorway pages) with anonymised/teaser professional info, and gate the full profile + contact behind the subscription. Resolve this contradiction explicitly with the client.

## Details — Spec Contradictions & Unresolved Decisions

Flag every one of these on the call:

1. **Pricing model:** base doc says "tarification automatique par type de profil"; annotation says "NON — roulette entre 100 et 300€." One table row literally contains both. → Confirm the €100–300 slider is final; delete automatic tariffs.
2. **Payment flow:** annotations strip it to service-fee-only with off-platform settlement, no escrow, no Connect, no mission-start code, no payment-release validation. → Confirm no money for the care service ever transits the platform. Biggest architectural and legal consequences.
3. **Verification:** cut in one version, reintroduced by annotation (itsme + ID card + manual diploma review + sworn declarations + transparency disclaimer). → Confirm verification is IN for V1 and agree the *manual* review workflow.
4. **Profile visibility vs SEO:** "SEO-indexable public profiles" vs "profiles visible only to paid subscribers." → Mutually exclusive; must be resolved.
5. **Subscriptions:** added late, "wanted from the outset," 4 family tiers + pro annual + gift cards — but **all prices TBD**. → You cannot build/price billing without numbers; a scope-and-revenue blocker.
6. **Profile data:** base doc has type/zone/availability/bio/preferences; annotation says "for now only LOCATION matters, no preferences." → Confirm minimal profile for V1.
7. **Matching modes:** both (a) listing + applications and (b) map browse + direct request are in scope. → Two full matching flows doubles the matching/UX work; defer one to V1.1.
8. **Messaging lifecycle:** thread auto-closes after the garde ends (consult-only history) — a specific, non-obvious rule to implement and test.
9. **Ratings:** stars only on 3–4 criteria, no written reviews, both sides — but public reviews are on the future roadmap. → Confirm no free-text in V1.
10. **Disputes:** routed to founders' email, not in-app — fine for V1, but define the (non-contractual) response-time expectation.

## Details — Effort Estimate (experienced solo full-stack, Next.js + Supabase)

Benchmarks: agencies quote €/$25k–70k for a two-sided marketplace MVP (8–12 weeks with a team). Belgian senior full-stack freelance day rates: community benchmarks put the average freelance day rate around **€590/day** (2022 survey), with senior full-stack/architect profiles commonly **€500–850/day**; Belgian senior hourly contract rates are quoted at €100–140/hr. Western-European senior contractors run broadly €500–800/day. I model at a blended **€600–750/day**.

Off-the-shelf (Sharetribe, Bubble) would cut listing/booking plumbing but cannot cleanly do itsme, the unusual fee-only payment split, gift cards, and manual diploma review — so the boilerplate saving is limited for *this* feature set, and you inherit lock-in and monthly fees.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — before you commit (this week).**
- Do NOT sign a fixed-price contract for the full spec. Propose a **paid discovery/architecture sprint (5–8 days)** to resolve the 10 contradictions, lock V1 scope, and produce a fixed V1 quote. This protects you from scope creep (the single biggest MVP budget killer) and demonstrates senior value immediately.
- Put three things in writing as **client responsibilities/blockers**: (1) itsme contract + pricing — they must open the sales conversation now (it can take "under 3 months"); (2) a legal opinion from *their* lawyers on childcare licensing (ONE/Kind & Gezin), collaborative-economy eligibility, DAC7, and the Platform Work Directive; (3) final subscription/gift-card/fee pricing. If these three are unresolved, V1 cannot responsibly launch.

**Stage 1 — recommended V1 (defensible ~45–70 days, ~€27k–€52k).** Cut to the smallest thing that tests the core hypothesis (do parents pay a fee to book vetted night pros?):
- **One matching mode only** — map browse + direct booking request (the differentiator) OR listing+apply, not both.
- Auth, minimal profiles, document upload, **manual** verification, transparency disclaimer, sworn checkboxes.
- Service fee via Stripe + cancellation retain/refund logic. **Defer full subscriptions and gift cards** to V1.1 unless pricing is locked (revenue-critical but not needed to validate demand).
- Request lifecycle, scoped messaging with auto-close, star ratings.
- **Admin back-office (non-deferrable** for a marketplace — you need moderation and verification tooling from day one).
- itsme if their contract lands in time; otherwise launch with ID-card + manual review and add itsme in V1.1.

**Stage 2 — V1.1+ (defer).** Second matching mode, full subscription tiers, gift cards, AI-assisted diploma triage, in-app disputes, admin notes, then roadmap items (native app, daytime care, geographic expansion, public reviews).

**Commercials.** Bill day-rate or milestone-based, not fixed-price-whole-scope. Quote V1 at ~45–70 days. Add a monthly retainer for post-launch iteration. Given likely limited funding, be prepared for Stage 1 only — which is the right call for them too.

**Thresholds that change the plan.**
- If itsme sales quote a high minimum or >3-month lead → launch without itsme, ID-card only, revisit later.
- If their lawyer flags childcare-accreditation or undeclared-work exposure as unresolved → do not launch the booking/payment flow; consider pivoting to a pure directory (weakens DAC7/collaborative-economy issues but also weakens the business).
- If subscription prices remain TBD at contract signing → carve subscriptions/gift cards out of V1 entirely.
- If they insist on fixed-price full scope with non-technical oversight and no discovery → **decline**; the scope-creep and dependency risk is too high for a solo dev.

**Where you add the most value (lead with this on the call).** Architecture and phasing; regulatory-aware product decisions (the payment/verification/Platform-Work-Directive choices are where a senior engineer prevents a legal own-goal); the admin/ops tooling the founders will actually operate; and AI-assisted document review — using Claude/OpenAI vision to pre-triage uploaded diplomas/certificates (extract issuer, name, date, flag anomalies) so the founders' manual review is faster and consistent, framed clearly as "assist, not decide." This directly serves their stated ambition to "automate later," and is differentiated consultant value beyond coding.

## Caveats
- **itsme pricing is not public**; the euro figures cited (reseller e-signature €5; €450k government dispute) are illustrative, not a startup quote. itsme cost is a genuine unknown until their sales team quotes it.
- **Regulatory conclusions require a Belgian lawyer.** My reading of the collaborative-economy regime (cash-off-platform likely disqualifies it), childcare licensing, DAC7 scope, and the Platform Work Directive is well-sourced analysis, not legal advice; the founders' lawyers must confirm before launch.
- **Market sizing is directional**, built from national birth statistics and comparator rates, not a bottom-up Belgian night-nanny demand study (none was found). The declining birth rate and heavily subsidised alternatives are headwinds I am confident about; the absolute willingness-to-pay at €100–300/night is uncertain.
- **Effort estimates assume an experienced solo dev on a known stack** and exclude visual design (brand provided) and content. They will balloon if both matching modes, full subscriptions, and gift cards are all forced into V1, or if the founders iterate heavily mid-build.
- No dominant francophone-Belgian competitor was found; this is either genuine white space or a signal the niche is too small to sustain a standalone platform. **Bsit adding night care is the most likely competitive threat** — it already has liquidity, itsme/Stripe ID verification, subscriptions, and local density.
- Some cost/benchmark figures come from vendor/agency blogs (marketing incentive to skew); ranges were cross-checked across multiple sources where possible.
